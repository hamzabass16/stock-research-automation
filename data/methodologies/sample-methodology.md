# Sample Investment Methodology — Quality Compounders

*A generic, illustrative judgment lens that ships with the public repository. Replace it with your own methodology through
**Manage** in the app; the research template never changes, only this lens does.*

## Philosophy

Long-term returns come from owning businesses whose intrinsic value compounds, bought at prices that do not already assume
perfection. Markets are directionally efficient but structurally imperfect: short time horizons, index flows and narrative
cycles create gaps between price and value that a patient, fundamentals-driven process can exploit.

## Research standard

- Primary sources first: 10-K, 10-Q, 8-K, earnings-call transcripts and investor-relations material. Aggregators are used only
  to cross-check figures already obtained from a filing; when they disagree, the filing governs.
- Every number in a report states the period it comes from (quarter or trailing twelve months).
- Opinions are anchored to the metrics below, never to sentiment alone.

## Quality thresholds (what "good" looks like)

| Metric | Threshold | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| ROIC / ROCE | ≥ 20% sustained | Capital efficiency is the dominant driver of long-run returns |
| Gross margin | ≥ 40% | Pricing power and a buffer against input-cost inflation |
| FCF conversion (FCF / net income) | ~100% | Profits that are real cash, not accruals |
| Interest coverage | > 15× | Not dependent on debt markets to operate |
| FCF per share growth | Positive and accelerating | The business compounds for shareholders, not just in aggregate |
| Revenue growth stability | Low coefficient of variation | Predictability deserves a premium |

## Archetypes

- **Compounding Machine** — passes at least five of the six thresholds above, with a durable moat (network effects, switching
  costs, pricing power, intellectual property or irreplaceable assets). Valuation: free-cash-flow yield relative to its own history
  and to the sector; pay a fair price for excellence, never a reckless one.
- **Dividend Growth Powerhouse** — ten or more years of rising dividends, payout ratio below 50%, five-year dividend growth above
  10%, diversified international revenue. Valuation: dividend yield versus history and the risk-free rate.
- **Disruptive Growth Stock** — organic revenue growth above 20% into a market larger than $10B while gaining share, with an
  industry-specific KPI that is outperforming. Valuation: EV/sales or EV/gross profit against a path to profitability; size
  positions small because the failure mode is total.

## Decision rules

- **Buy** when the archetype test passes, the thesis is specific and testable, and the valuation offers an asymmetric payoff
  (projected upside at least twice the projected downside over the time horizon).
- **Buy at Better Valuation** when the business qualifies but the price already discounts the thesis.
- **Hold** when the thesis is intact but the edge has narrowed.
- **Avoid** when a quality threshold fails structurally, when management allocates capital poorly (value-destroying M&A,
  dilution above 3% a year, cutting R&D to hit short-term targets), or when the thesis cannot be falsified.
- **Kill signal**: every recommendation names the one observable event that would invalidate the thesis and force an exit.

## Statistical discipline

A thesis is only credible if history shows the variable it depends on has moved this stock. Test it with abnormal returns
(after removing market and sector effects), state the hypothesis, the test, the p-value and the sample size, and report honestly
when the data does not support the story.
